# Staff: the roster, as designed

Agreed over several passes. The thesis is the last line of the brief that produced it:
**you are managing people in a lab.** Specimens are the subject, people are the game.

## Roster classes

One roster. Everyone is a staff record with the same shape, and a role decides what they
are for. Roles open up as the station grows, so the opening hours stay simple.

| Role | Opens | Expert for | Only they can |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lab tech | start | nothing; two are needed for any hands-on work | |
| Xenobiologist | start | sampling, assays, biochemistry, extraction | |
| Zoologist | start | observation, habitat, sedative clearance, provocation | |
| Microbiologist | Micro Lab built | spread tests, revival, screening, vaccines | |
| Geneticist | Genetics Lab built | sequencing, cloning, crop strains | hybridization |
| Chemist | Chem Lab built | synthesis, derivative search | |
| Physicist | Physics Lab built | spectroscopy, stress, batteries, shielding, horizon | |
| Containment engineer | second containment cell | containment challenges; slows integrity loss station-wide | |
| Speech researcher | something vocal on station | acoustic analysis | signal decoding |
| Xenoanthropologist | ruins or tools recovered, or sapience found | dating, signal decoding | argue a sapience finding |

The expert effect is three-sided, which is what makes hiring legible:
- the expert **on the crew**: faster, cleaner data, lower risk
- an expert **on station** but not on the job: no bonus
- **nobody on station** who understands the work: markedly more unreliable data

Only a few things are hard-gated (hybridization, signal decoding). Everything else degrades
rather than closing, so a thin roster never soft-locks a run.

## Lab heads

Each lab is run by its own specialist, and opening a lab means hiring the person to run it:
the build is refused until somebody qualified is free, and the reason says who to hire.
Containment cells are a xenobiology post; each specialty lab is headed by its own
discipline. Heads reseat themselves when the roster changes, so a death, a walkout or a
dismissal leaves a lab unheaded until it is filled. A built lab with nobody at its head
still runs: **35% slower, and half again as likely to file a finding as unreliable**, with
the lead naming the empty post as the reason.

Once the station holds **two labs of the same type**, the two heads are in each other's
professional space, and that is where the people problems start. Each same-discipline pair
carries a tension and a bond that drift daily on the two heads' traits and morale: two
ambitious people, or a glory hound beside anyone, climb fast; a quiet or steady head keeps
it calm. Four things come out of that, all of them at the evening briefing and all of them
decisions rather than notifications:

- **Authorship dispute** at moderate tension. Name a lead author and the other one takes it
  badly, or split the credit and disappoint both.
- **Sabotage** at high tension. Bea names who has been in whose lab out of hours. Move them
  off rotation, dismiss them, or do nothing and watch a lab lose integrity overnight with
  no reason filed.
- **A corporate spy**, placed at hire once the station has published something worth
  stealing. Calderón's audit finds the leak. Dismiss them, leave them in place and feed
  them false numbers, or do nothing and lose standing.
- **An attachment** at high warmth. Morale rises in both labs and they cover for each
  other, including the near misses. Separating them costs both of them.

None of it is a relationship screen. It arrives as people telling you about people.

## Stats

Four disciplines, scored 0 to 4 and shown as pips. Three map onto the work modes the
scheduler already has; the fourth is what holds when it goes wrong.

- **Handling** hands-on work with live specimens. Fewer incidents, fewer breaches.
- **Bench** precision work. Quicker, and less unreliable data.
- **Observation** patience at the glass. Quicker, and catches confounds.
- **Nerve** composure when containment fails. Decides who walks out of the cell.

Every hire has a **potential** per discipline as well as a current score, so a bad hire stays
a bad hire. Experience accrues per discipline from hours actually worked, which makes keeping
people alive a mechanical advantage and not just a moral one. Nerve is the one stat that can
fall: surviving a breach scars it.

## Traits

Every trait gives something and costs something. Several decide who a person can stand
working under, which is what gives the lead choice teeth beyond its risk multiplier.

Examples as built: `Fast hands` (15% quicker, markedly more incidents, likes Vasko),
`By the book` (never skips decon, 12% slower, likes Okafor and clashes with Vasko),
`Squeamish` (unshakeable at the bench, refuses hands-on work), `Glory hound` (fast, cuts
corners, clashes with Okafor), `Green` (learns fast, unreliable until the hours are in),
`Veteran` (steady under pressure, will not be told how to work by a new lead).

## Morale and friction

Morale moves daily on affinity with the lead, injuries, hazard pay and whether an ambitious
person is being given the work they want. Low morale costs a pip on every discipline except
Nerve. Bea raises a person's grievance once, with a choice: move them to bench work only, or
keep them on full rotation and risk losing them. At zero they take the shuttle.

## Assignment

Auto-assign with override. Every item gets the best available crew for the discipline it
needs, so a six-lab day stays playable; a crew chip on each planner block lets the player
override where it matters, and a named crew always wins. Anyone named but hurt or missing at
start time is backfilled rather than blocking the work.

## Budget

The station is R&D, not a business, and the science never stops for accounting.
Meridian funds the work up front with a grant large enough to stand several labs up at
once, and **nothing is ever refused for want of credits**: no study, lab, mission, hire,
repair or overtime. Spending past the grant puts the station **over budget**, which is
recorded rather than prevented. Work approved into the red is logged as such, the funds
chip turns red, and every day in deficit costs standing on a scale that grows with the
size of the hole. There is no bankruptcy ending; a program that stays in the red long
enough is cancelled for lost confidence, which is the same door as too many retractions
and too many bodies.
